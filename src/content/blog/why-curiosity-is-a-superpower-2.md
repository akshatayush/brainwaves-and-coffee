---
title: "Why Curiosity Is Actually a Superpower Too"
date: 2026-03-06
description: "We're told to specialize, to niche down, to pick a lane. But what if the most interesting people are the ones who refused?"
tags: ["curiosity", "thinking"]
---

There's a version of me that exists in a parallel universe who picked one thing and got very, very good at it. He's probably more productive. He maybe has fewer browser tabs open.

I am not him.

The thing about being a curious person is that you spend a lot of time feeling like you're doing something wrong. You should be deeper into machine learning by now. You should have finished that philosophy book. You should not have spent three hours last Tuesday reading about how ancient Romans made concrete.

But here's what I've come to believe: the breadth *is* the depth.

## The unexpected connections

The most interesting ideas don't live inside a single domain. They live at the borders. The moment you notice that the way a murmuration of starlings moves looks suspiciously like how traffic jams propagate — that's not a distraction. That's your brain doing something genuinely rare.

Curiosity is how you collect the raw material. Specialization is how you process it. But without the wide-eyed, slightly embarrassing enthusiasm for *everything*, you run out of inputs.

## On rabbit holes

I've made peace with the rabbit hole. Yes, I started reading about the French Revolution and ended up on a Wikipedia page about the etymology of the word "guillotine" at 1am. This is fine. This is, arguably, the point.

The information you absorb from a rabbit hole isn't wasted even if you never "use" it in a conventional sense. It becomes part of how you see the world. It becomes a lens.

## The only advice I have

Stay curious. Follow the thing that makes you say *huh, that's interesting* even when it seems irrelevant. Especially when it seems irrelevant.

That's it. That's the whole post. Go read about something weird.
