Every morning I grind the beans before I do anything else.

Not because the grind-to-brew gap matters that much (it does, a little). But because the sound of the grinder is an alarm clock for a different part of my brain. The productive part. The *here we go* part.

There's something worth examining in why rituals work at all. We don't need the ritual. The coffee would taste roughly the same if I bought it pre-ground. My brain would get its caffeine either way. But it wouldn't feel the same. And that feeling — the specific anticipatory pleasure of the kettle heating while I measure out the beans — is doing real psychological work.

## On transitions

One thing I've noticed: the hardest part of any creative or focused work isn't the work itself. It's the transition *into* the work. Getting yourself across the threshold from "person who is not working" to "person who is working."

Rituals are transition technologies. The coffee ritual is a reliable, repeatable bridge. When I pick up the grinder, some part of me starts warming up the part of my brain that does the actual thinking. By the time the coffee is ready, I'm ready.

## Caffeine aside

I wonder sometimes if I'd miss the ritual even if the coffee had no caffeine in it. I think I would.

The warmth. The smell. The few minutes where nothing is required of me except to stand there and watch water heat up. It's a tiny, daily permission to slow down before speeding up.

That's probably worth more than the caffeine.
